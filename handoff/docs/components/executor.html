The training executor and the pipeline adapter

After this page you can say what a job compiles to, how stages are scheduled and guarded, how the executor reports to the hub, what the pipeline pin is and how it moves.

Last verified 2026-10-20 · CONTRACT v1.39 · owner: ai-training-web

What it is for

The executor runs training for the hub. It is a service on the platform host (:8900 production, :8901 dev) with no screen and no login: people start, watch, cancel, resume and deploy jobs on the hub's 학습 (training) pages, and the hub calls the executor's /exec/v1 API for them (CONTRACT §9). Opening the executor's address in a browser sends you to the hub.

  • Who you are comes from the hub: every request carries a short-lived hub service token naming the hub user and role.
  • Whose files a job writes is decided by a binding: an admin links each hub user to a NAS account on 관리 › 계정 연결 (account links). The job's files land under /mnt/<account>/, owned by that account, and its stages run in that account's workspace container. A hub user with no binding can look but not start anything. Each row of the binding list (GET /exec/v1/bindings) also shows the account's uid/gid and workspace_ok — whether that account's workspace container is running, the same check as GET /exec/v1/accounts (since 2026-10-18; before, the hub showed it empty). The can train flag only gates a labeler starting a job under their own name; jobs the hub sends as app:hub are not checked against it.
  • Policies (automatic training) are the hub's: the hub decides when a policy fires and sends the job as the service user app:hub, which is bound to a NAS account like any person.

Jobs, templates and stages

A job is a template plus its parameters plus a cohort. The executor turns it into an ordered list of stages and runs them one after another.

Training defaults are Research's (v1.38 §53, D233): the train block of hub_loop and once carries no values of its own. The executor instantiates TrainingConfig from the pinned clone's class definitions in a subprocess that writes no bytecode (the file's own example instance reads a NAS fingerprint, so it is not imported whole) and offers its resolved fields — epochs, workers, optimizer and scheduler classes, the three validation settings — as the parameter defaults with source: "research"; GET /exec/v1/templates adds research: {commit, path}, and answers 503 research_defaults_unavailable rather than invent values when the clone cannot be read. Only train.n_gpus, train.gpu_model, train.push and the wait's timeout and polling are source: "executor", each with its reason in the help. train.batch_size is the one TrainingConfig field the executor sets itself (v1.39 §53.3, U88, D251): 2 in every template, whatever the mode, source: "executor" (researchdefaults.EXECUTOR_TRAIN) — Research's 8 ran out of CUDA memory on the RTX A6000 for jobs 62, 65 and 66; the user may raise it, and an out-of-memory still halves from the job's own value (§48.2). The example template keeps its own 10 epochs (marked executor). See the training guide for the values at the pin.

The deploy step in a job (v1.38 §54, D234): hub_loop and once end in a deploy_model step after the evaluation, switched by deploy.enabled (hub_loop on, once off) with deploy.model_name, deploy.version, deploy.research_rois, deploy.memo — the hub fills name, next version and memo. steps lists it with optional: true, switch: "deploy.enabled". A job body that sends no deploy.enabled deploys only when it names a model (deploy.model_name), so the hub's loop jobs from before v1.38 deploy nothing. Refused at creation, not after the training: an empty name, version or memo (422), a version that exists (409 deploy_version_exists), a person's own token without the deploy role (403; the hub's app:hub decides for its presser). The stage runs only when every earlier stage succeeded — the evaluation included — does what POST /jobs/{id}/deploy does (the same deploy_model.sh call, the same §3 deploys registration with the job's account) and is titled Deploy <name> <version> on the job page. The route itself is unchanged (D68).

Which RTSTRUCTs a project job trains on (v1.39 §53.1, D242): exactly the project's label revisions the hub names, no fall-back; one the download did not bring is fetched by its UIDs from the hub's label store, then from ONCOPACS (C-FIND by series UID + C-GET, AITW_PACS_*, D248), and one found nowhere is left out and named in missing[] and the log (§53.2, D247); only an empty set fails the select stage; convert.mode is fixed for hub-cohort templates (422 fixed parameter convert.mode for another value); the job view's rtss_selection {mode, n_pinned, n_kept, as_of?, missing[]} says what was kept (details).

Class names (v1.34 §48): when the run uses the team's ROI registry, the target ROIs in start.rois are trained under the registry's official names, mapped through the run's own snapshot (runs/<id>/roi_registry.csv); a name the registry does not know stays as sent. classes.json, the model's ROI names, the evaluation's per_roi keys and the pre-label ROI names are therefore official names; the hub matches them back through its alias map. GET /exec/v1/info says so with capabilities.official_class_names. Before this (job 60, 2026-10-18) a target sent in an alternative spelling was silently dropped by preprocess.

v1.41 (CONTRACT §61.1, U109, D269) — the wizard's own items. On a hub_project cohort, cohort.series (up to 4000 image series; a series cohort stays at 500) is a restriction: the resolved label-set keeps only the listed series. A listed series the label-set does not hold (no usable label in the requested states) is skipped and named in cohort_resolved.series_unknown; cohort_resolved.n_series is the number kept. Nothing else changes: the label states and as-of, the pinned RTSTRUCT revisions (§53.1), the label fetch (§53.2), the ROI subset (§45.3), the max_patients cap, and valid_pinned (a pinned series outside the kept set still refuses the job, 422 valid_pinned_missing). The hub sends train ∪ valid as series and the valid rows as valid_pinned, and does so only while GET /exec/v1/info reports capabilities.series_restriction:true. Without the list the job is the whole label-set, byte for byte as before.

v1.43 (CONTRACT §61.1 amendment, U112, D271) — a restricted cohort is pruned by series uid. Prod jobs 70–73 (Thoracic_LN, 2026-10-23) lost 24 of 50 cases before the converter: the select stage's store step hid patients outside the cohort by the hub's patient id (CT-C-005) while the store is keyed by the DICOM PatientID (63beed22…). Now a hub_project cohort with series is pruned by image series uid (_prune_series, as a series cohort) and the store and the downloader get the DICOM PatientIDs of the kept items. Where that id comes from, per image series: ONCOPACS's own index first (scpdb.series_summary, the PatientID the downloader selects and files the store by), then the hub's series[].patient_id (DICOM, v1.43), then the label-set's patient_id (as before; scpdb unreachable is said in the log). This holds for every hub_project cohort, restricted or not: after the 2026-10-23 reconcile the label-set itself gives the hub's id for 27 of Thoracic_LN's 55 items (it is the PatientName), so the unrestricted path had the same fault. A label the select stage fetches (§41.8, §53.2) is filed under the folder that holds its image series. The select log reads series cohort: <kept> of <n> requested series found and DICOM PatientID per series: scpdb n, hub series n, label-set n, plus the hub ids that differ (CT-C-005 → 63beed22…); GET …/patients lists the DICOM ids.

v1.58 (CONTRACT §75.3, U149, D326–D327) — hub_items: items chosen anywhere. The hub's From items anywhere wizard picks labelled image series from any project, from no project and from the central SCP, each with the label it chose, and sends them inline: cohort {kind:"hub_items", rois:[…] (required), items:[{image_series_uid, patient_id (DICOM, required), study_uid?, rtss_series_uid, rtss_sop_uid?, label_file?, label_state?, label_kind?, project_code?}] (1–4000, unique series), valid_pinned?}, valid_pinned ⊆ the items' series. projects, series, patients, upto_cursor, as_of and label_states are refused with this kind (422 naming the field). hub_loop, hub_checkpoint, once and example list it in cohort_kinds (and so every §55 pipeline built on them); prelabel_only does not. The executor asks the hub nothing: the inline list is the label-set — the start block becomes hub_items whatever the template starts with, and compiles to the same pinned select as a project's (§53.1) with the same entries (image_series_uid, rtss_series_uid, rtss_sop_uid, label_state, label_kind, label_file, patient_id), pruned by series uid (as v1.43); classes = ["background"] + rois; the patients are the DICOM PatientIDs (scpdb first, the item's second — the select log says DICOM PatientID per series: scpdb n, hub items n); max_patients is ignored (the list is the choice). label_file:true items are fetched from the hub (§41.8), PACS-held ones come with the download or by series UID from ONCOPACS, and a pinned item found nowhere is left out and named (§53.2). No project: project_code must be null (422 otherwise), the in-flight scope is the hub's job (items:<hub_job_id>), and registrations carry no project_code. A template that names a model family needs deploy.model_name (422 hub_items needs deploy.model_name; hub_checkpoint has none and needs none); the pre-label hand-off is off and the target-predict branch is not compiled (prelabel.hub_push:true → 422 no project to push pre-labels to). cohort_resolved gains kind:"hub_items", n_series, projects:[codes], central_only_n (items with no project_code) and label_states; the job detail gains projects. GET /exec/v1/info reports capabilities.hub_items:true; the contract mock (tests/mock_exec.py) takes and refuses the same bodies. Every other cohort kind compiles as before (test_items_anywhere.py).

v1.60 (CONTRACT §76.2, U151, D329) — hub_vouches: a person without a NAS binding runs under the hub's binding. POST /exec/v1/jobs with the hub's token (sub=app:hub) and requested_by: "user:<name>": when that person has no NAS binding, the job runs under the app:hub binding (the hub's NAS account, as the loop's and the auto-contour jobs do), requested_by stays the person and no can_train check applies — the hub's token vouches. A bound person keeps their own account; a person's own token is unchanged (binding + can_train or a deploy role, else 409 unbound). Cancel / stop-training / resume / export keep accepting the requester, the hub's token and the deploy roles: the person's own token cancels or stops the job the hub submitted for them (sub = the job's requested_by); a resume of such a job goes through the hub's token, which vouches again, while the unbound person's own token is refused it (403 "not bound", as before). Since every vouched person shares the hub's NAS account, a vouched job without a project is in flight per person (scope nas:<hub account>|user:<name>), so two labelers' jobs never refuse each other; the create audit row carries vouched_by: app:hub. No vouch without an app:hub binding (409 unbound). Code: execauth.run_as, used by jobs.create and resume; the hub-facing mock (tests/mock_exec.py) answers the same. GET /exec/v1/info capabilities carry hub_vouches. E23.

v1.61 (CONTRACT §77.3, U152, D333) — the default pipeline is the latest released bundle; candidates are listed, never defaulted. A folder of /mnt/deploy/release is a release when its name is x.y.z or the fix form x.y.z.n, ordered by the numeric parts (1.7.0 < 1.7.0.3 < 1.7.1 < 1.10.0); site builds (smu_1.3.0) are not. Its state is the state of its RELEASE.json (written by ai-pipeline's packager, §77.2: candidate → released → superseded, or withdrawn); a folder without the file — every package before 2026-11-05 — is released; a file that does not parse or names no known state reads unreadable and is never the default. GET /exec/v1/info.default_pipeline = {release, releases:[V…] (every folder, oldest first), states:{V: state}, candidates:[V…] (newest first), models, derived_rois} with release = the highest folder whose state is released and models / derived_rois read from it; folders but none released → release: null, empty models, the list still answered; null without the share. The mtime watch covers every folder's RELEASE.json, so a state flip shows within one snapshot refresh (≤ 60 s on the units, E22b) without a restart. prelabel_only's release accepts any release folder, a candidate too (QA runs it on purpose); a withdrawn one → 422 {reason: "release_withdrawn"}. The executor only reads the share. contract_version is "1.61". The hub-facing mock answers the prod share after A15 (1.7.0 released, 1.7.0.3 candidate). Code: release.state / latest_released / default_pipeline, jobs.resolve_models. E24.

Classes to train (v1.41 §61.3): every training template (hub_loop, once, example and every pipeline built on them) has train.class_mode, relevant (default, source: executor) or all. relevant is Research's relevant-classes training, unchanged: each item trains only the ROIs its structure set holds, and the other classes are masked out of the loss and the Dice. In all mode, just before the training is submitted, the executor rewrites each item's resampled.class_index_to_name in the preprocessed resampled_fingerprint.json to every class of the job's classes.json. That file is the trainer's json_dir, and Research's train/dataset.py reads the mapping from there and only there: the npz files hold only arrays, and train_list.json / valid_list.json are the converter's NIfTI lists. The mask channels are already in global class order and a missing ROI's channel is empty, so an ROI an item lacks is taught as absent (background). The background class itself is left in the mapping only where the item already had it: the converter builds a background channel only for an item that holds every class (dicom_to_nifti.py:724-729), so for the others channel 0 is all zeros and teaching it would teach "nowhere is background" (E3 audit, U112); the log counts those items. Research is not edited. The train stage's log states the mode, and in all mode how many items lacked at least one class. GET /exec/v1/jobs/{id} carries class_mode (null for a job that does not train). The rewrite is idempotent, so an out-of-memory resubmission (§48.2) or a resume is safe.

The parameter form the hub shows is generated from the template (GET /exec/v1/templates); every parameter is named <step>.<field>, for example train.num_train_epochs.

The cohort and its label states

For a hub project the executor asks the hub for the project's labels (CONTRACT §6.12): the latest label per image series among the label states you chose, as of a point in time. The five states are approved, unreviewed, changes_requested, prelabel (AI pre-label) and legacy (imported). Nothing is refused because of its state; leaving the states out means all five, now. The RTSTRUCT chosen for each series is pinned: only that structure set is converted, whatever else was downloaded.

Several models in one job: model_paths and release (v1.21, CONTRACT §28.2)

prelabel_only takes the model in exactly one of three forms, else 422 {reason:"model_params"} with one sentence saying which forms were sent:

A folder's modality is its global_fingerprint.json modality field (any case — 1.7.0's Chest says CT); without the field, an MR_ name prefix means MR. Its version is the fingerprint's git_tag (model/ct/Abdomen/release_2.0.0 → 2.0.0). requested_by (optional) names the person the hub acted for: it is echoed in the job's params and ends the RTSTRUCT's StructureSetDescription as · by <user>.

Only the project's ROIs: roi_filter (U20, D171) — a JSON list string of ROI names as the project names them (after roi_rename; a hub hand-off renames nothing). With release only the models that own a listed ROI run (the owner map above), so a two-ROI project runs two models, not sixteen; with model_paths / model_path the listed models all run. Either way the RTSTRUCT keeps only the listed ROIs. A listed name no run model makes is recorded as rois_unavailable on the job; when none of them can be made the job is refused at creation, 422 {reason:"roi_filter_empty"} (a malformed list: 422 {reason:"roi_filter"}). results[].models_skipped names the release models left out. Either spelling (v1.34 §48): the filter and the models' class names are both compared in the ROI registry's official names — at creation through the registry as the run will take it, in staging through the run's own snapshot — so a filter CTVn_L2_L keeps the model class CTVn_LN_Ax_L2_L, and an official name in the filter finds an older model's alternative spelling. rois_unavailable lists names as they were sent.

How it runs. One download and one conversion per series, then one predict stage per model, one after another (each its own GPU stage under the admission lock — never two models loaded at once — each writing its own predictions/), then one staging step that builds one RTSTRUCT per series: every ROI is taken from one model only. With release that is its owner in the release's default_roi_presets.json (CT: the entry's default_model; MR: the MRI template's, or the entry's own for an MR-only entry); an owner of the wrong modality or one that lacks the class (1.7.0: Anal_Canal's CT entry names MR_Pelvic) falls back to the first model that has it, and the log says so. With model_paths the first listed model wins. Every drop is in the staging log. Derived ROIs (generation_type 2) are not produced. One failing model never voids the job (§28.2 amendment): with several models each predict stage is advisory — a model that fails is left out, the next one runs, the RTSTRUCT is written from the models that ran (an ROI the failed model owned comes from the next model that has it), and only staging with no prediction at all fails the job. Device: the check step reads each model's TorchScript (CPU, in the person's workspace) and gives every predict stage this run's own model folder (runs/<job>/models/NN_<family>/): a link to the model, or — for a model whose code never moves its input to the GPU, which the pinned predict entry point hands a CPU tensor — a copy with its weights on the CPU, so it runs on the CPU. In 1.7.0 that is External 2.1.0 only (ExternalSliceModelFilled; without the copy: Input type (torch.FloatTensor) and weight type (torch.cuda.FloatTensor) should be the same). Release 1.7.0 CT: 16 models, 236 model ROIs, 231 kept, 5 dropped (BrachialPlex_L/R from Breast → HNN, Humerus_L/R from TotalBone → Chest, V_Pulmonary from Heart → TotalVessels); MR: 6 models, 87 of 114 kept. Peak GPU memory: see below.

ROI names (v1.22, CONTRACT §29.1, U27). A pre-label handed to the hub (hub_push: hub_loop, hub_checkpoint, prelabel_only) names its ROIs exactly as the pipeline emits them — the official names; until v1.21 each got a _pre suffix. No prefix and no roi_rename on a hand-off, so roi_filter speaks the official names. The file is told from a human set by its marking only (StructureSetLabel = AI_PRELABEL, SeriesDescription = AI_PRELABEL …): push_prelabel refuses an unmarked file, and choosing an RTSTRUCT for training hides a marked one whatever its ROI names. Files stored before v1.22 keep their _pre names.

The training loop (v1.28, CONTRACT §41.4–41.5). The hub's Train is an ordinary hub_loop job submitted as app:hub; the research pipeline, the adapter and the templates are unchanged. Two things the hub relies on: (1) the artifact event {name:"model", path, md5} is sent right after the export stage's stage_finished, before the evaluate stage's, even when one sweep sees both finish — the hub starts the iteration's prelabel_only job (model_job_id) on it; (2) POST /exec/v1/jobs/{id}/deploy with requested_by:"app:hub" under the hub's service token is accepted like a lead's (token role admin or lead; app:hub is bound to a NAS account like a person) and records requested_by and the memo on the deploy. The hub picks <next> from /mnt/deploy/models/<name> itself (it mounts the share read-only); an existing version is still 409 deploy_version_exists. info.contract_version = "1.28" (1.23–1.27 changed nothing on /exec/v1).

The iteration's own pre-labels off (v1.28 §41.3, D210). hub_loop and hub_checkpoint list prelabel.hub_push (bool, default true) in /exec/v1/templates; false drops the whole target-predict branch (Predict and Pre-label: predict, staging, nii→DICOM, label, hand-off) — the job ends with export, evaluate, report — and its select log starts with hub_push off: no pre-label hand-off and no target-predict branch in this job. The loop's pre-labels come from prelabel_only on the export artifact. prelabel_only does not list it (sending it is 422). train.push is the trainer image push, always done and ignored when false.

Labels held by the hub (v1.28 §41.8, D205). Since v1.24 (§34) a label saved in OncoStudio lives on the hub and reaches the central SCP only when a lead sends the approved file, so the patient-scoped download does not bring it (dev ZZ_LOOP job 0b6a371d: PELVIS_2023_0022 missing, a pinned validation item, the split refused). The select stage of every hub-cohort job (hub_loop, hub_checkpoint, the wizard) first fetches each label-set item marked label_file:true whose RTSTRUCT is not in the store from GET /svc/v1/labels/{rtss_sop_uid} (aitw service token, application/dicom, the transport de-identified file) to where the downloader would have put it, dicom/<PatientID>/<image series>/RTSS_<rtss series>.dcm (mode 644; checked: the SOP UID must be the one asked for). The log says ↓ P/series RTSTRUCT sop: fetched from the hub (n bytes); a file already in the store with that SOP is not fetched again. A fetch that fails (404, an HTML page, a different SOP) is named in the log (✗ … not fetched from the hub — reason) and fails the stage — never dropped. The research argv is unchanged.

A pre-label a person saved is a human label (v1.28 §41.11, D209). The hub's record wins over the file's marking: a structure set whose SOP the hub's label-set names as label_kind: human is kept even when it still carries AI_PRELABEL (a pre-label accepted or corrected in OncoStudio before the app re-stamped such saves) — log ✓ <patient>/<series> kept: the hub records a human save of this AI_PRELABEL file. The marking hides every file the hub did not name; the newest-with-class mode (no hub record) is unchanged. Dev case: ZZ_LOOP_LIVER job f2250ccc, P000016.

The job view adds results, one row per series: {image_series_uid, patient_id, models_run:[family], models_failed:[{family, version, error}], models_skipped:[family], rois, rois_dropped:[{roi, from, kept_from}]} (empty until staging ran; error = the last error line of that model's predict log). The pre-label hand-off (§7.3) carries the first model's md5 and version; the RTSTRUCT names the whole set — SeriesDescription AI_PRELABEL default pipeline 1.7.0 CT (16 models) or AI_PRELABEL Abdomen 2.0.0 + Chest 2.0.1, StructureSetDescription the same text with round <n> · before and · by <user> after. A prelabel_only job's in-flight scope is project + modality, so the hub's CT and MR jobs of one request run side by side.

Run layout — every job's outputs are its own (D275, E5). Under /mnt/<user>/oncostudio/ each output of a job lives in <kind>/<organ>__<graph>/<job id>/: data/ (NIfTI, dataset.json, classes.json, train_valid_dict.json, previews), preprocessed/ (npz, fingerprints — the trainer's json_dir), models_export/…/<job id>/3d_final_model.pt (the file the hub's model registration and the release bundle point at, and the deploy stage's model_dir), eval/, predictions/, prelabel/…/{staging,targets,rtstruct}. Checkpoints were per job already: models/<organ>/<organ>_<job id>/. Before D275 these were per graph, so the next job of the same graph rewrote the last one's inputs, metrics and exported model — prod job 69 rewrote job 65's data and npz, and the trainer reads each npz on every access, so a rewrite under a running training changes what it trains on. Jobs created before the change keep their old paths (stage params are frozen at creation). The grow / reuse split modes read the graph's newest earlier split (data/<organ>/*/train_valid_dict.json, else the pre-D275 data/<organ>/train_valid_dict.json). Size of one Thoracic_LN job (49 CT, 18 classes): data 2.1 GB, preprocessed 2.2 GB, checkpoints 0.3–0.5 GB, export 84 MB, eval 2 MB, snapshot 37 MB ≈ 4.8 GB; nothing is deleted by the executor (retention: orchestrator decision pending).

The smear guard — an input rule (U112, D272; permanent since U113b, D278). A structure set whose contours of a trained ROI carry no ContourImageSequence is an unsupported RTSTRUCT: the executor does not train on it. This is the rule for what the pipeline accepts, not a stopgap waiting for a fix. The research converter paints such a contour on every slice of the image (dicom_to_nifti.py:610-624: slice_idx stays None, the referenced branch is inverted, mask_3d[None] ^= mask_2d), at 1ef108b and at 767b753 alike, and no fix is coming upstream. Measured at 767b753: Chest_2023_0002's LN_Thorax_10_R lands on 187 of its 187 slices, while CT-C-005's (with the sequence) lands on 3 of 138. The sequence is optional in DICOM and the OncoPlan-written Chest_2023 / QIN-BREAST files omit it: prod job 65 trained on 12 such cases out of 44, 71% of its station voxels. Scheduling, lanes, locks, durability

  • Lanes. Every stage waits for a slot in its lane: net 2, cpu 2, gpu 1, service 1, and one active stage per NAS account — except the hub's own jobs (app:hub: the training loop's iteration and its pre-label job, D207), which may run two (AITW_MAX_PER_USER_SERVICE, default 2) beside that account's own one; the GPU lane stays 1, so the two never load models at once. The limits follow the host's 1 Gb/s NAS link, not its cores — more parallel stages would make each one slower.
  • The GPU is shared with ai-pipeline through one admission lock; a GPU stage also waits until the host has 12 GB of free memory. The stage log says so while it waits.
  • Output locks. Converting and preprocessing delete their own output folder first, so a stage holds its output folder exclusively; two jobs never write the same folder at once. The conversion also holds the account's DICOM store (<store>/dicom) until it ends: which structure sets it reads is decided by hiding files there, so another job's download or selection waits (D207).
  • Durable stages. Each stage runs as its own background unit on the host, so restarting the executor does not stop a running stage: after a restart it picks the stage up again. A busy executor database does not lose a stage either (since 2026-10-18): saving how far its log was read is best effort, and its outcome is retried until it is written.
  • A full cluster: the training pends in Ray (v1.38, §48.1, D238 — 2026-10-20): a one-GPU training (the templates' default) is sent to the Ray cluster at once and waits in Ray's own queue, in cluster order with other people's jobs — it is never refused or timed out for capacity. Research's submit_task.py checks free capacity itself and pins a job to the node it picked, so with no free node nothing would reach Ray; the executor instead sends Research's own command for the job (its _submit_single_node call at the pinned commit, used read-only) with the same GPUs, CPUs per GPU (32, never lowered) and RTX_A6000 resource and without the node pin (backend/app/raysubmit.py; a test checks the command equals Research's). The stage log records the equivalent submit_task.py call, then pending on the cluster: <ray id> (requested 1 × RTX_A6000, 32 CPUs per GPU); Ray ids are aitw_<job>_s<stage>_<UTC time>. Submit training stays running with stages[].pending: true (and ray_job_id, submitted_via: ray_direct) on GET /exec/v1/jobs/{id}, gives its service lane back meanwhile, and succeeds when Ray starts the job — so Wait for training's deadline counts from the Ray start. If Ray fails or stops the job before it starts, the stage fails with ray_job_failed before it started: …. An executor restart re-attaches the stage to its Ray job by id (never a second submit). Cancel asks Ray to stop the job (stop_job); see the note below on what Ray 2.38 does with a pending one.
  • More than one GPU: the old capacity wait (v1.35, §48 amended; deprecated, kept only until Research's launcher has a pending form, D238): a request over one GPU still goes through submit_task.py. When Ray refuses the training submit for capacity (Not enough RTX_A6000 capacity at 32 CPUs per GPU …), Submit training stays running, logs waiting for cluster capacity: <the cluster's answer> and tries again every 2 minutes; from 30 minutes of waiting it asks for fewer CPUs per GPU, one step per half hour (32 → 24 → 16, never below 16, logged; a job placed lower says submitted at cpus-per-gpu N), and it gives up after 6 hours with cluster_capacity_timeout. The image is built once. Between attempts the waiting stage gives its service lane and its owner count back (D224) — other jobs' split, submit and pre-label hand-off run meanwhile — and takes the lane again only for each attempt, admitted like any queued stage. An executor restart during the wait queues the submit again (its last attempt was refused, so no Ray job exists); one cut off in the middle of an attempt still fails, because Ray may have taken it. Cancel stops the waiting within seconds.
  • Waiting for training holds no lane (v1.38): Wait for training only polls Ray, for up to wait.timeout_hours (336 h in the templates, for Research's 1000 epochs), so it gives its service lane and owner count back for the whole wait, as the capacity wait does (D224). It keeps the Ray job id on its own row and re-attaches after an executor restart (§54 amended, D236): the log says re-attached to <ray job> after an executor restart, and the deadline still counts from the first start. A resume clears the stored id, so a wait after a re-run submit follows the new Ray job.
  • A CUDA out-of-memory retries at half the batch size (v1.39 §48.2, D249 — 2026-10-21): when the Ray training ends FAILED with OutOfMemoryError / CUDA out of memory in its status message or the tail of its driver log, Wait for training does not fail. It builds the training again with batch_size halved (floor, min 1; patch size and everything else as the submit stage had them: same data, split, experiment name and output folder) under an image tag of its own (…_oom<n> — Research's docker run has no --pull, so a reused tag would start the node's cached image at the old size), sends it to Ray as the submit does (it may pend, §48.1), and waits on the new Ray job. At most three retries (8 → 4 → 2 → 1); the fourth out-of-memory, or one at batch size 1, fails the stage with the out-of-memory line itself as its error. The log says CUDA out of memory in <ray job> at batch_size 8: retry 1 of 3 at batch_size 4. The retries are kept on the wait's row (a restart counts the ones made; a resume clears them) and on GET /exec/v1/jobs/{id} as oom_retries: [{at, batch_size_from, batch_size_to}] (at RFC 3339 UTC, 2026-10-20T21:58:52Z, like every executor timestamp; retries recorded before 2026-10-21 read …+00:00Z on the row and are served normalized), with params_changed: {"train.batch_size": {value, reason: "cuda_oom"}} = the size the training last ran at. Each retry is an event training_retried. Cancel stops the current Ray job; a cancel during the rebuild sends nothing. Every other training error fails as before. Evidence: hub job 62 (d02fda5b), batch 8, 45.8 GiB of 47.4 GiB allocated by PyTorch in epoch 1.
  • Cancel (POST /exec/v1/jobs/{id}/cancel; v1.39, §58, D241) stops the job wherever it is: queued stages are dropped; a running executor stage is signalled and, if still there 30 s later, killed; a Submit training pending in Ray gets stop_job (§48.1); a training on the cluster (Wait for training) gets stop_job at once, then its follower waits up to 30 s for Ray's STOPPED and runs a small Ray job pinned to the training's node that stops any container of the run's image still there (Research's docker run has no name; the image tag oncosoft-trainer:<experiment>_<run> is unique to the run) — the log lists what it found and ends cancelled by <user>: <ray job id> stopped. No NAS binding is needed to cancel (the binding is for starting jobs): with the hub's own token the hub has checked requester / project lead / admin, and the user it names is recorded. The job shows cancelled_by and cancelled_at; a finished job answers 409 already finished. The old capacity wait stops its retry within seconds. Resume re-runs a chosen stage and everything after it on the same job — same folders, same split, same pipeline commit — and is refused while an earlier stage has not succeeded.
  • Stop after this epoch (v1.48 §69, D301, E19 — 2026-10-27) ends a training that has saturated and goes on to export, unlike Cancel. The pinned trainer (767b753) has no stop hook of its own (no stop file, signal handler or config flag: its epoch loop ends only on its own full-validation patience), so the executor stops at an epoch boundary it can see. Two signals, one effect: POST /exec/v1/jobs/{id}/stop-training {requested_by, reason?} (same rule as Cancel: requester, lead or admin; the hub's own token needs no binding), or a file stop_training.json = {"stop": true, "by": "…", "at": "…", "reason"?: "…"} that a person writes into the run directory (/mnt/<user>/oncostudio/runs/<job id>/), read by Wait for training at each poll. The stop is recorded on the wait's row and the job (stop_requested_by, stop_requested_at) with the epoch that must finish: the one running now, i.e. the last row of the trainer's training_log.json + 1. The trainer rewrites that file after each epoch's patch and full validation and their checkpoint saves, so when the row for that epoch appears the epoch is complete and its checkpoints are on disk; meanwhile the wait polls every 5 s. Then it stops the Ray job exactly as Cancel does (Ray STOPPED confirmed, the node checked for a container of the run's image), but the stage succeeds with stop_reason: manual_stop on the stage and the job. The trainer's own end (model.pth, final_results.json) never ran, so Export takes the best checkpoint on disk — checkpoints/best_full_model.pth, else best_patch_model.pth, else the newest checkpoints/*.pth (same {model, config, global_fingerprint} format) — and its log says which (checkpoint: the best full-validation checkpoint (best_full_model.pth) of <experiment> — no model.pth, …); evaluate, report and the deploy rule follow as after early stopping (nothing downstream reads final_results.json, so none is made up). The wait's log reads stop requested by <who>: the training ends when epoch <n> has completed (§69), epoch <n> completed: stopping <ray job>, Ray: STOPPED, stop_reason manual_stop: …. A second request answers the first one's record (already_requested: true). Refused with 409: a job not in Wait for training (not_training) and a training with no completed epoch (no_epoch_completed — there would be no checkpoint to export). A file is renamed once acted on (stop_training.applied.json / .refused.json), so a later resume that trains again is not stopped by it; a file that is not JSON or says "stop": false is left alone. A restart during the wait still stops after the recorded epoch; a resume clears the stop. GET /exec/v1/info.capabilities.manual_stop is true.
  • Previews and reports are advisory: if one fails, the job goes on. Their pages open from the job page (GET /exec/v1/jobs/{id}/stages/{stage}/page).

Events and registrations

The executor tells the hub what happened in two ways, both through an outbox stored in its own database and retried with back-off — a hub outage delays them, it never fails a stage.

  • Job events (POST /svc/v1/exec-events, §9.2): job_created, stage_started, stage_finished, job_finished, job_cancelled, artifact (split, model — right after the export, §41.4 —, metrics, curve available, snapshot) and pipeline_verified; in order, up to 100 per call. The hub also polls each unfinished job every 30 s. training_retried {reason: "cuda_oom", batch_size_from, batch_size_to, attempt} (§48.2) is sent on its own, outside the ordered batches: a hub that does not know the kind yet refuses the whole call (422), so it is marked skipped and holds nothing back — the hub then reads oom_retries on the job.
  • Facets (§11.6, v1.5): after every conversion an allow-listed row per series from the converter's index goes to POST /svc/v1/cases/meta (source pipeline_db) — no names or birth dates; skipped and logged without the capability, on a hub before 1.5, or on a 404/422 (the outbox parks the batch as skipped).
  • Pre-labels (§7.3) are handed over synchronously by the push_prelabel stage, not through the outbox: a refusal fails the stage where a person sees it — except 422 series is in the validation set, which skips that file and is counted in the stage log (D89).
  • Registrations (§3): the split (with each item's label_state when the cohort came from label-set; the split hash does not change — D75), the run, the model (md5 of the exported weights), the run evaluation and deploys.

The model's type (v1.47 §63). Every job that trains or checks a model has a model_type (3d | 2d: its train.mode, or the training stage's mode for a job from before): in the job_created event and the model artifact events (event detail is open, so any hub reads past it), and in the model and deploy registrations to a hub that reports contract 1.47 or later (an older one refuses an unknown key, so it gets the old body).

The curve with the full validation (v1.48 §66, D298, E17). GET /exec/v1/jobs/{id}/curve answers the rows as before, [{epoch, train_loss?, valid_loss?, valid_dsc?, full_valid_dsc?}]; full_valid_dsc is on the epochs where the pinned trainer's sliding-window validation ran (every train.full_eval_interval, 50 by default). The list stays the default body, because a v1.47 hub draws every numeric key of a list but nothing from an object. With ?detail=1 the answer is {rows, best: {epoch, full_valid_dsc} | null, stop_reason}, where stop_reason is early_stopping | epoch_limit | manual_stop | running, or null (no training yet, or it failed or was cancelled). GET /exec/v1/jobs/{id} carries the same best and stop_reason. Where the numbers come from: training_log.json holds only the running best_full_valid_dice, so each full validation's value is read from the trainer's TensorBoard file (<experiment>/tensorboard/events.out.tfevents.*, tag full_eval/dice_mean at the global step, mapped to the epoch by the log's step). It is read by a small reader of our own (app/tfevents.py; no tensorboard in the venv) and cached per file version. Without the file only the improving epochs are known. best is the first epoch with the highest value. stop_reason comes from the wait (manual_stop, running), or, for a finished training, from comparing its last epoch with num_train_epochs in the experiment's configs.json. Thinning to 500 points keeps every full-validation row. The run registration's metrics_summary carries best and stop_reason beside curve (datasets).

params_changed for a mode default (v1.48 §68, E18): a job whose train.mode is not 3d and whose request left out a mode-dependent parameter shows the value it got: params_changed: {"train.batch_size": {value: 16, reason: "mode_default", mode: "2d"}}. The value is read off the job's resolved parameters and the request as sent. A §48.2 retry overrides it with reason: "cuda_oom". See training for default_by_mode.

A manual stop (v1.48 §69) is one event training_stop_requested per request (detail {stop_requested_by, after_epoch, via: api | file, reason?}), sent on its own like training_retried so a hub that does not know the kind yet (422) holds nothing up; the wait's stage_finished then carries stop_requested_by, stop_requested_at and stop_reason, and job_finished carries stop_reason. GET /exec/v1/jobs/{id} has stop_requested_by, stop_requested_at, stop_reason.

A stuck delivery is visible at GET /api/hub/status (hub token): counts per state and the last error of each pending row.

Evaluation slices for the job page's viewer (v1.39 §56.1, D239). GET /exec/v1/jobs/{id}/eval/cases lists the cases of the job's evaluation (stem, shape and spacing, slices per plane, DSC / HD95 per class, modality) and the classes in the model's channel order with their overlay colours. GET …/eval/cases/{i}/slices/{plane}/{k}.png?layer=image|gt|pred&window=soft|lung|bone|mr is one layer of one slice: the greyscale image (CT window, default soft 400/40; MR the 1–99 percentile of the volume) or a transparent overlay with one colour per class. Planes: axial, sagittal, coronal (the latter two stretched to square pixels). The executor renders a slice the first time it is asked for and keeps it under the run, runs/<id>/eval_preview/<i>/<plane>/<k>_<layer>[_<window>].png; after the first uncached slice of a (case, plane, layer, window) the rest of that stack is rendered in the background (§56.4, below). Three rules worth knowing: the slices are shown the radiological way (anterior up, the patient's right on the left) after reorienting each volume by its header, not as stored; a class is matched by name between the case's mask and the model's output (their channel orders can differ), so a structure has the same colour in GT and prediction; and only this job's files are shown (below). Answers: 404 no evaluation (no succeeded evaluate stage), 404 for a case or slice out of range, 410 evaluation files gone, 416 for a plane that is not served, 422 for an unknown layer or window.

Prerendered samples and the fast path (v1.45 §56.4, U115, D279). The report block's eval_prerender_n (template parameter report.eval_prerender_n, default 5, min 0, title Prerendered evaluation samples) is the number of cases the report step renders whole: after the report files are written, inside the same stage (CPU lane, never the GPU), N case positions are drawn at random with the job id as seed (N ≥ cases → all), and every axial slice of each is rendered — image in the default window (soft CT, mr MR), gt, pred — one volume read per layer, one log line per case, then eval_preview/prerendered.json {n_requested, cases:[i…], at, seconds}. Advisory (§56.1): any failure is logged (eval prerender: skipped — …) and the report stage still succeeds; a report compiled before v1.45 renders 5. eval/cases carries prerendered: [i…] and cases[].prerendered (only while the cache's stamp is this evaluation's). A slice request is answered from the cached file without reading any NIfTI and without waiting on the render pool; a cold slice is rendered from its own layer's volume only, then the whole stack of that (case, plane, layer, window) is queued on a background pool of two workers (at most six stacks waiting, the newest first, the oldest dropped; a finished stack leaves a .complete_<layer>[_<window>] marker).

The cold path, the warm-up, the palette (E9b, D280). A mask or prediction is a 4-D one-hot file (Thoracic_LN: 512×512×148×19, 737 MB unpacked); it is streamed once and folded one channel at a time into a 3-D label volume, never held whole, and that volume is kept compressed beside the slices (eval_preview/<i>/vol_<gt|pred>_<tag>.npz, ~60 kB) so a later process reads it in ~0.1 s. On job 65 the first slice of an untouched case went from 20.5 s (all three volumes before any slice) to 1.1 s (image), ~4 s (gt / pred). After the report's N samples the other cases' axial stacks are rendered by one background thread at nice 19, outside the volume LRU; the report stage does not wait for it, and it stops at shutdown, when a stage of the job is cancelled, or when the cache's stamp changes. Progress is prerendered.json warmed {state: running|done|stopped|failed, cases:[i…], total, at, seconds?}; eval/cases keeps prerendered = the N random cases. eval_prerender_n: 0 renders nothing and warms nothing. Overlay colours: 24 (the first 8 as before), one per class index, so up to 24 classes never share a colour.

The pipeline adapter (pin, verify, update)

The executor never edits or imports the research pipeline (Oncostudio-Research) directly. One package in its repo, pipeline_adapter/, holds everything that knows it: the entry points and their exact arguments, the configuration files the pipeline reads, the names of its output files, and what each pipeline version can do.

  • The pin — pipeline_adapter/pipeline.lock: the research commit and its code tree, the container image stages run in, when it was verified and by whom. The hub's 파이프라인 (pipeline) page shows it. Since 2026-10-24 (E7b, D278) it is Research 767b753 (origin/development HEAD, tree f9db6f3), image oncosoft-pipeline:767b753-full-nii2dcm, contract 2; before that 1ef108b (tree f3e9c27, image 40bc7f2-full-nii2dcm, contract 1). What moved for our stages: conversion, preprocessing and evaluation code is unchanged (data_utils/ and validation/ are identical), but the image is CUDA 12.8 with torch 2.8, monai 1.4.0 and numpy < 2 (it runs on this host's driver 535). The trainer no longer copies the whole preprocessed dataset first (copy_dir is gone). It fetches each volume on first use from the folder that holds json_dir, and caches the deterministic transform prefix in the container's cache/, which it refuses to fill past 90%. Research's Ray docker run now caps memory at shm_per_gpu × GPUs (90 GB) with half of that as /dev/shm (it was all /dev/shm), also mounts /mnt2, and removes the image afterwards. Classes with zero area get weight 0 instead of inf, the train Dice mean is a nanmean, and the 3d-ax head is larger (9 decoder layers, feed-forward 1024).
  • Verify — python -m pipeline_adapter.verify: every entry point answers inside the image, the image's code is byte-identical to the pinned tree, the generated configs compile, and a one-patient fixture goes through convert → split → preprocess → export → evaluate. The quick form (--static-only) takes under a second; the full run a few minutes and the GPU.
  • What a red verify blocks: new jobs and resumes (pipeline_unverified, with the reason). Running jobs are not touched.
  • Moving the pin — pipeline_adapter/PIPELINE_UPDATE.md: pull the research code, build and push the image, verify, update the lock, restart the executor, check the hub's pipeline page is green. The research code itself is never edited from here.
  • Capabilities: what the pinned pipeline can do, declared per contract version and checked by verify — e.g. db_sidecar (the converter's DICOM index the facet harvest reads, v1.5), multi_rtss_aggregation (false: --mode all is refused), dataset_copy_dir (contract 1 only: the generated training_config.py passes copy_dir only where the pin's dataset config has it — under contract 2 that argument would stop the training at import), mode_2d (v1.47 §63, contracts 1 and 2: "2d" in training_config.py's mode Literal and its Auto2DDefaultConfig — train.mode 2d is accepted only with it; recorded in the lock since 2026-10-27).
  • Hidden dependency (D69): at the current pin every export loads a personal checkpoint file at import time; verify checks it still exists, and the pipeline page lists it.

The adapter code is maintained together with the pipeline-catalog role (it knows the research code best); this section is written by the executor.

What can go wrong

A job waits in queued for a long timeits lane is busy, the account already has a stage running, or its output folder is held by another job → the job page says which stage is waiting; one account runs one stage at a time on purpose (the hub's own jobs two).
A GPU stage sits at "waiting for memory"the host has less than 12 GB free, or ai-pipeline holds the GPU lock → it starts by itself when both clear.
The cohort is smaller than expectedthe chosen label states or as_of exclude labels, or max_patients cut it → compare label_states in the job with the hub's cohort preview.
"NVML Unknown Error" in a GPU stagea host service reload revoked the container's GPU → the executor restarts the workspace once by itself; if it repeats, an administrator stops the reload.
422 model_paramsa prelabel_only job named no model form or two of them, release without modality, a release folder that is not there, or model_paths of two modalities → send exactly one form; the sentence names what arrived.
A stage shows running but its log stopped growingits follower died (before 2026-10-18: a database is locked in the executor's log) while the unit ran on → backend/logs/durable/stage-<id>.exit holds the real exit code; an executor restart, or recover's finish path for that one stage, records it and the job continues. Never re-run the finished stages.
Submit training says "pending on the cluster"no A6000 node has a GPU and 32 CPUs free → nothing to do: the job is in Ray's queue (the Ray dashboard lists it as PENDING under its aitw_… id) and starts in cluster order; the stage succeeds then. A cancelled pending job: Ray 2.38 cannot drop a job whose supervisor has not been placed — stop_job is accepted, the dashboard keeps showing it PENDING, and when a node frees Ray starts it and stops it at once (STOPPED; accepted, D238). The executor's stage is cancelled immediately and its log says cancelled: <ray id> stop requested; Ray ends it when it starts.
Submit training says "waiting for cluster capacity" (a request over one GPU only)the Ray cluster has no node with enough free CPUs beside a free GPU → nothing to do: it retries every 2 min and lowers cpus-per-gpu after 30 min; after 6 h it fails with cluster_capacity_timeout. A job that failed at Submit training (or whose wait never started) is resumed at that stage — POST /exec/v1/jobs/{id}/resume with its stage_id — reusing its converted and preprocessed data.
The evaluation viewer answers 410 evaluation files gonethe run's evaluation files were cleaned away, or (reason evaluation_files_replaced) a later job evaluated the same data: the evaluate stage wrote to a folder keyed by the dataset (eval/<organ>__<graph>) for jobs before D275, so the next evaluation overwrote it, and showing those masks under this job's DSC would mislead → nothing to fix: the job's metrics stay readable from its snapshot, only its pictures are gone with the files. Jobs since D275 evaluate into their own folder (run layout).
Events look late on the hubthe hub was unreachable and the outbox is retrying → /api/hub/status; the hub's 30-second poll fills the job page meanwhile.
